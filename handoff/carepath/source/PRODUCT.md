# CarePath
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform
web

## Users and purpose
Adults65+ in the US understanding unfamiliar medical paperwork; caregivers are a separate secondary audience. Recruitment will include both audiences. Help people interpret a fictional hospital letter, check its source, prepare questions, and request human review.

## Constraints
Independent portfolio concept. Scripted AI; no real file uploads, medical records, contact details, income amounts, eligibility decisions, application submissions or external messages. Retain the clear/unclear paths, correction, explicit sharing consent, frozen handoff snapshot and withdrawal. Research has not yet been conducted.

## User commitments
Lead with CarePath and its concrete purpose. Remove advertising slogans, long prose and small text. Use the supplied rounded-card mobile references, contextual imagery, large labelled controls, an interactive iPhone frame, and synchronized interaction logic outside the device. Main phone copy20px+, primary controls60px+, larger-text option. Proposed first research round:4 older adults and2 caregivers in separate sessions. Actual research language and geography remain to be confirmed.

Portfolio copy addresses recruiters and other reviewers. The approved scope is medical-document understanding and support preparation. Make the five product decisions visible in a mind map after project metadata and in compact image categories with a full-width expandable explanation; remove conversational notes addressed to the designer.

## Caregiver collaboration prototype
The older adult selects a fictional daughter, chooses access to one document/checklist, separately permits checklist assistance, and explicitly confirms. Caregiver controls and views are gated by scope and active status. Owner revocation hides even an already-open caregiver document, preserves owner progress and requires a new owner confirmation before further access. This is local simulated authorization, not production identity or access infrastructure.

The portfolio homepage must explain caregiver collaboration through three visible stages: older-adult authorization, scoped caregiver viewing/assistance, and revocation. Each stage names what each person can do and how access changes; the demonstration is part of the homepage, not only navigation.
